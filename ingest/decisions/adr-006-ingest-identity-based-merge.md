# adr-006 — One purchase, one row: identity-based merge across SMS, email and statements

- **Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-20 · **Tags:** curated, cat:reliability · **Supersedes:** adr-005 · **Iteration:** 198 (HP3, H3, M4)

## Context
A purchase can arrive as a bank SMS, a bank alert email and a statement line. v2 (adr-005) only paired SMS with statement lines, ran hourly, and refused any SMS whose *category* was transfer-like. Categories are guesses, so 132 card purchases (Rs 1,29,474) were counted twice; it also merged nothing by bank reference, and the batch SMS path de-duplicated on amount+date+merchant, losing genuine repeat payments.

## Decision
Every source writes through `modules.txn_store.insert_transaction`, idempotent on (source, source_ref), which links the new row immediately via `modules.merge`. Identity is decided only from facts about the money movement: normalised card, direction, amount, date and bank reference — never the category. Rules, strongest first: `ref`, `same-body`, `unique` (1:1 pairing), `merchant` (corroboration), `tolerance` (surcharge / FX, needs card + merchant). Two rows from the same source never merge except by reference or identical body. Statement line > SMS > email decides which copy stays live; losers keep `is_duplicate=1`, `matched_txn_id`, `merge_rule`.

## Consequences
- The 132 double-counted purchases collapse; bank alert emails can become a transaction source without double counting.
- Genuine repeats survive (different reference numbers are proof of different transactions).
- Every link records the rule that made it, so a wrong merge is diagnosable.
- Readers are unchanged: they already filter `is_duplicate = 0`.

## Amendment — 2026-10-20 (same iteration, after the staging rehearsal and three verifier rounds)
Rehearsing on a copy of the live DB showed that SMS-to-SMS copies need rules of their own, and that a rule judged only on "same card, amount, minutes apart" merges real purchases. The rule set is now:
- `ref` — identical bank reference, any source, ±3 days. A reference of 10+ characters (UTR / RRN) links even when the two copies name the account differently (a bank's UPI-ATM SMS and its alert email print different numbers for one account, same RRN).
- `same-body` — identical text received within 2 minutes (one alert delivered twice). Identical alerts minutes apart are two purchases: SBI's card alert carries no time or reference.
- `repeat-alert` — a second **alert family** for one charge within 5 minutes: spend alert + e-mandate / standing-instruction alert, or two sender families. Two bodies of one template are copies only when identical; the numbers that differ (auth time, reference, balance) are what tells two purchases apart.
- `twin-alert` — one account transfer reported by two SMS sender families (SBI YONO + core banking) within 10 minutes; within 3 minutes their texts need not agree (YONO names the account, core banking the person).
- `same-alert` — the same for bank alert emails: two email templates for one movement within 10 minutes.
- `unique`, `merchant`, `tolerance` — as above.
- `count` — identical purchases no rule can tell apart are paired 1:1 in time order: k SMS / email rows with k of n ≥ k statement lines within ±1 day (a statement lists every purchase, so the live count never drops below its lines), or equal numbers of SMS and email on one day.

Vetoes that apply to every SMS link: a different reference (read from the SMS text when `ref_no` is empty; `backfill_sms_refs` stores them for legacy rows), or a different time of day, available balance / limit or counterparty VPA printed in the bodies. Every rule — `ref` included, because a group's winner can carry a reference inherited from a copy — links two rows only when **both groups are compatible member by member** (the new row's own group and the candidate's, not only their winners), on the full alert text: different references, a different printed balance or time of day (between an SMS and an email, the time with 5 s of slack: real copies differ by at most 2 s, two purchases were 35 s apart), or two different bodies of one template from one sender (a bank reports one transaction once per template) mean two transactions. An alert email first looks for the row carrying its exact reference; only then does `same-alert` (modules/email/bank_alerts.py) try every twin candidate until one passes the same check. Where several candidates fit, `merchant`, `repeat-alert`, `same-body` and `twin-alert` take the one closest in time. Links the old matcher made that put two transactions in one group are undone once at deploy (`repair_inconsistent_groups`) and re-made by the sweep; a legacy SMS row whose SMS record is gone is linked to its one same-day twin (`link_orphan_copies`, rule `orphan-copy`). A row is never linked to itself; the post-deploy report counts duplicates that point at themselves, a missing row or another duplicate (must be 0).

## Amendment — 2026-10-21 (iteration-198 re-verify, rounds 8–9, shipped with iteration 203)
Probes of two same-amount transfers, each seen by any mix of YONO / core-banking SMS and mails, showed guesses taken against evidence. The rules now separate **proof** from **guesses**:
- **Proof first.** A printed balance or time of day shared with a group is proof (rule `proof`). An alert email batch is stored first, then linked in the order the bank *sent* the mails: pass 1 links only on proof — its exact reference, then the live group that prints its balance / time (`merge.proof_target`); pass 2 takes the nearer of its closest compatible twin and `find_match`'s copy. In `find_match`, when several candidates remain, the one group that prints the row's balance / time wins before `merchant` and `count` (it comes after `ref`, the SMS sibling rules and `unique`). After `find_match` and the release below, `link_on_insert` and the sweep fall back to `proof_target`.
- **One alert per sender family**, not one email per SMS: a YONO mail and a core-banking mail both belong to their transfer's group when proof puts them there. A member that sits in a group only as a far guess proves nothing about it.
- **Far guesses yield to evidence.** An SMS ↔ email pair more than 10 minutes apart is refused only when a nearer alert provably belongs to another transaction and the two print no shared balance / time — distance alone never refuses (41 genuine live pairs are 10 min–44 h apart: late HDFC card mails, ICICI reversal mails, an SBI standing-instruction mail 40 min before its SMS). When a row finds no match, a weakly linked (`unique` / `merchant`) far copy in its way, or a `same-alert` twin whose own balance / time the row's group prints, is released and both re-pair with what is near; a twin is released only when the row's group can take it. A `proof` link evicts a same-source far guess from the group (a transfer's mails come seconds apart, not hours). No reference is inherited across a far guess.
- **Payees.** Two account-transfer alerts whose *transfer wording* names different beneficiaries ("for transfer to <name> Ac", "-Transferred to Mr. <NAME>.", "Beneficiary Name <name>") are two movements, for `unique` / `count` / `tolerance` and for `same-alert` twins (which are tried closest in send time first). Narrations ("towards annual maintenance charges") and the parser's fallback ("SBI ACCOUNT TRANSACTION") are not names.
- **History.** `scripts/iter203_f198_steps.py` `step_far_guess_repair` undoes existing far guesses today's rules refuse, removes references stamped across them and re-sweeps (0 changes on the 2026-10-20 live copy).
- **Residual, accepted.** When one transfer is seen only by YONO-family alerts and another only by core-banking alerts to the same payee, no printed fact tells them apart; such pairs can still merge (probe: 106 of 2,700 same-payee runs). Two transfers to different payees, and every case a balance, time or reference settles, come out right.
